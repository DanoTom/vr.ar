The Best VR Games (2026)

Updated June 2026 · Curated list

This is a curated — and inevitably subjective — pick of the most notable, relevant VR games in 2026. We weighed overall quality, innovation, staying power and availability across the main platforms (Quest 3/3S, PSVR2, PCVR/Steam, Pico). Prices are approximate and vary by platform, edition and region.

Half-Life: Alyx

9.6/10
PCVR/Steam Moderate $59.99

The game that proved what VR can really do.

Half-Life: Alyx is still the benchmark — the title people point to when they explain why VR matters. Valve built a full-length, big-budget Half-Life around the medium's strengths: handling objects, solving physical puzzles, leaning around cover. If you have the PC for it, it's the first game to play and the one everything else is measured against.

Best for: Anyone with a capable PC who wants the single best showcase of what VR can be.

Action, Adventure, FPS

Where to play: Steam →

Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 (PSVR2)

9.5/10
PS5 + PSVR2 Intense $69.99 Multiplayer

Fly the world's skies with overwhelming realism on PSVR2.

More than a "VR game," MSFS 2024 on PSVR2 is a gigantic simulator with a full VR mode. The scale of the world, the cockpit wrapped around you, the sound and the sheer sense of being there make it almost meditative — until things go wrong. It's demanding on both hardware and stomach: best for people already comfortable in VR who enjoy the act of flying more than "beating levels."

Best for: Aviation enthusiasts after graphical realism and deep simulation.

Simulation, Flight

Where to play: PlayStation Store →

Beat Saber

9.3/10
Quest 3/3SPSVR2PCVR/Steam Comfortable $29.99

The rhythm game that became VR's calling card.

Beat Saber is the easiest game in the world to understand and one of the hardest to put down: slash blocks to the beat with two lightsabers. It's the perfect first VR experience, a genuine workout in disguise, and endlessly replayable thanks to custom songs and music packs. Almost nobody bounces off it.

Best for: Newcomers, music lovers, anyone who wants the definitive "show your friends VR" game.

Rhythm, Music

Where to play: Steam →Meta Quest Store →

Batman: Arkham Shadow

9.2/10
Quest 3/3S Moderate $49.99

Become the Dark Knight and defend Gotham in this epic VR adventure.

Arkham Shadow nails the fantasy of being Batman: a combat system that reads your body and stealth sections that make you feel like a real caped vigilante. It's a must for anyone who grew up with the original Arkham games and wants to feel the weight of the cowl first-hand. Not the comfiest for marathon sessions, but as a VR superhero experience it's front-line.

Best for: Batman fans, action-adventure lovers, anyone after an immersive superhero experience.

Action, Adventure, Superhero

Where to play: Meta Quest Store →

Aces of Thunder

9/10
PSVR2PCVR/Steam Intense $29.99 Multiplayer

Pilot historic warplanes in intense VR aerial battles.

Aces of Thunder leans closer to "serious" simulation than arcade: less about watching gauges, more about feeling the aircraft around you. It's a great example of how VR can transform your relationship with speed, vertigo and distance in a dogfight. If your fantasy is glancing through the canopy at the sun between your wings while someone's on your tail, this is your place.

Best for: Flight-sim enthusiasts, fans of historic dogfights, players who enjoy hard-to-learn, deeply rewarding games.

Simulation, Air Combat, War

Where to play: PlayStation Store →Steam →

Walkabout Mini Golf

9/10
Quest 3/3SPSVR2PCVR/SteamPico Comfortable $14.99 Multiplayer

The most quietly beloved social game in VR.

Walkabout Mini Golf sounds modest and turns out to be one of VR's best multiplayer experiences. The courses are gorgeous and imaginative, the physics are perfect, and chatting your way around a round with friends anywhere in the world is pure, low-stress joy. It's comfortable enough for absolutely anyone, and the cheapest "I get VR now" moment you can buy.

Best for: Couples, families, friends across distance — anyone who wants relaxed, social VR.

Casual, Sports, Social

Where to play: Steam →

Pistol Whip

9/10
Quest 3/3SPSVR2PCVR/Steam Moderate $29.99

John Wick meets a rhythm game — and it rules.

Pistol Whip fuses an on-rails shooter with a rhythm game and a heavy dose of action-movie cool. You duck, dodge and fire to the beat, and on a good run you feel like the most stylish person alive. It's accessible, intensely replayable, and one of the best "just one more level" games on any headset.

Best for: Players who want action and music together; fans of John Wick-style cool.

Rhythm, Shooter

Where to play: Steam →

Roboquest VR

8.9/10
Quest 2/3/3SPSVR2PCVR/Steam Moderate $19.99 Multiplayer

A frantic, fluid roguelite FPS rebuilt for virtual reality.

Roboquest VR takes the original's base — nimble shooting, wild builds, colourful style — and pushes it somewhere you literally have to aim and reload. It's adrenaline and rhythm, with runs that beg for build-and-weapon min-maxing. Perfect if you miss that "arena shooter" feel but want your body in the game too.

Best for: Fans of fast FPS, roguelite lovers, anyone who lives for the flow of moving and shooting nonstop.

FPS, Roguelite, Action, Co-op

Where to play: Meta Quest Store →Steam →

Little Nightmares VR: Altered Echoes

8.8/10
PSVR2Quest 2/3/3SPCVR/Steam Comfortable $29.99

Sink into an unsettling world and solve puzzles in this dark VR adventure.

Altered Echoes drags the series' sickly aesthetic into a perspective that makes everything feel enormous, heavy, almost tactile. It's ideal if you like horror built on a diffuse sense of dread rather than constant jump-scares. The pacing is slower than most VR games, which keeps it relatively comfortable even for newcomers.

Best for: Puzzle lovers, fans of atmospheric horror, anyone after a compact, distinctive story.

Adventure, Puzzle, Horror, Platformer

Where to play: Steam →Meta Quest Store →

Gorilla Tag

8.8/10
Quest 3/3SPCVR/Steam Intense Free Multiplayer

The free, arm-powered tag game that became a phenomenon.

Gorilla Tag has no sticks and no movement buttons — you run and climb by swinging your arms, and somehow that became one of the biggest social phenomena in VR. It's free, absurdly fun in a full lobby, and a real workout. The locomotion can be intense for newcomers' stomachs and shoulders, but few games capture VR's social magic like this.

Best for: Social players, younger crowds, anyone who wants a free, hilarious way to get moving.

Social, Multiplayer, Action

Where to play: Steam →Meta Quest Store →

One More Delve

8.7/10
Quest 3/3SPCVR/Steam Moderate $13.99 Multiplayer

Explore procedurally generated dungeons with friends in this co-op roguelite.

One More Delve is one of those indies that gets what makes a dungeon crawler fun: short loops, loot that feels meaningful, and physics that give every swing weight. Co-op adds a social layer that pairs nicely with the constant risk of permadeath. If you like swords, dungeons and talking builds while dodging axes, it's a shoo-in for your library.

Best for: Roguelite lovers, classic dungeon-crawler fans, small groups who live for "one more run."

Roguelite, Dungeon Crawler, Co-op, RPG

Where to play: Steam →Meta Quest Store →

Superhot VR

8.7/10
Quest 3/3SPSVR2PCVR/Steam Moderate $24.99

Time moves only when you move — VR's perfect gimmick.

Superhot VR's hook — time only moves when you do — was made for virtual reality. You become a slow-motion action hero, dodging bullets and snatching weapons out of the air in choreography you invent on the fly. It's short and stylish, and few games make you feel as cool as this one does.

Best for: Players who love a clever hook; anyone who wants to feel like an action hero.

Action, Puzzle, Shooter

Where to play: Steam →

Beat the Beats

8.6/10
Quest 3/3SPico 4PSVR2PCVR/Steam Comfortable $12.99

Box to the beat in this energetic VR fitness game.

Beat the Beats builds on "real" boxing moves (jab, hook, uppercut, rolls) wrapped in rhythm-game language. It's less abstract than hitting blocks and closer to a boxercise class, with tracks and remixes that pull you back for another round. A really good gateway for turning VR into a daily workout without losing the playful side.

Best for: Rhythm-game lovers, fitness fans, anyone who wants to sweat without noticing they're exercising.

Rhythm, Fitness, Music, Boxing

Where to play: Meta Quest Store →

TMNT: Empire City

8.5/10
Quest 3/3SPCVR/SteamPico Moderate $24.99 Multiplayer

Join the Turtles in an action-packed co-op beat 'em up built for VR.

Empire City is a love letter to Saturday-morning cartoons run through a VR filter. Punching, blocking and moving through the city has a physicality that grabs you fast, and in co-op it turns into a carnival. It doesn't pretend to be a deep RPG: it's a game to jump in, swing some nunchaku, laugh, and walk out with sore shoulders.

Best for: TMNT fans, groups of friends after direct, physical co-op, players who'd rather crack heads than micromanage builds.

Action, Beat 'em up, Co-op

Where to play: Meta Quest Store →Steam →

Wrath VR: Aeon of Ruin – Brutal Edition

8.5/10
Quest 2/3/3SPSVR2PCVR/Steam Moderate $29.99

A brutal retro FPS that hurls you back to the golden age of shooters.

Wrath VR: Aeon of Ruin – Brutal Edition is a very physical reading of the Quake era: oversized weapons, accelerated mobility, blood everywhere. The VR adaptation adds physical reloads, dual-wielding and a kind of movement that keeps you aware of your body and your real space. If you grew up on '90s FPS, this plays like a fever dream come true.

Best for: Fans of classic FPS, boomer-shooter lovers, players who want to move fast and shoot faster.

FPS, Retro, Boomer Shooter, Action

Where to play: Steam →Meta Quest Store →

Bootstrap Island

8.3/10
PCVR/SteamPSVR2 (soon) Moderate $29.99

Fight to survive on a mysterious island full of danger and secrets.

Bootstrap Island is, in a sense, a shipwreck simulator for the mind: it strands you alone on a hostile island and forces you to learn by dying. VR amps up the precariousness — lighting a fire, hunting, or just finding shelter feel like small victories. It's demanding, yes, but also a great example of how body and memory intertwine when the world turns tactile.

Best for: Fans of realistic survival, anyone who enjoys poking at systems over and over until they master them.

Note: Playable on Quest via PC Link/Air Link; no standalone Quest version yet.

Survival, Adventure, Open World

Where to play: Steam →

The Boys: Trigger Warning

8.2/10
Quest 3/3SPSVR2 Moderate $29.99

Unleash chaos in The Boys universe in this explosive VR adventure.

Trigger Warning understands its universe doesn't work if you "clean it up": there's blood, cynicism and pitch-black humour, and it's all here in VR too. The story puts you on the side of the not-so-saintly and lets you inhabit a powered body in a world that no longer believes in innocence. If the show made you uncomfortable, this game probably will too.

Best for: The Boys fans, players after a cynical, bloody superhero story, FPS lovers who want personality.

Action, Superhero, FPS

Where to play: Meta Quest Store →

FlatOut 4: Total Insanity VR

8.1/10
PCVR/Steam (Early Access) Intense $19.99 Multiplayer

Explosive racing and total destruction in this VR remake of FlatOut.

FlatOut 4: Total Insanity VR takes the childhood fantasy of crashing cars "on purpose" and puts it right in front of your eyes. The result is chaotic, loud and — when it all clicks — hugely cathartic. This isn't a game that bets on restraint: it's here to remind you VR can be pure arcade excess too.

Best for: Arcade-racing fans, vehicular-destruction lovers, players who want to see the crashes up close.

Racing, Destruction, Action

Where to play: Steam →

Squingle Arcade

8/10
Quest 3/3SPCVR/SteamPicoApple Vision Pro Comfortable Free

A free cosmic puzzler that pulls you into a meditative flow.

Squingle Arcade is almost a playable art installation: lights, shapes and music combine into a loop of gentle focus. It's great for introducing VR without overwhelming or nauseating anyone, and for always having something on hand that doesn't require "gamer mode." It's one of those titles you appreciate after an intense session in other worlds.

Best for: Casual players, fans of spatial puzzles, anyone after something meditative rather than competitive.

Puzzle, Casual, Relaxation

Where to play: Meta Quest Store →

VMX

7.9/10
PCVR/Steam (Early Access)Quest (in development) Intense $14.99 Multiplayer

Feel the rush of extreme sports in VR with BMX, scooter and mountain bike.

VMX is basically a bike-and-scooter park strapped to your headset: gestures, spins and tricks translate into physical movements that demand coordination. Even in Early Access it offers enough maps and modes to get lost in, though you can tell it's still being built. If you're into extreme sports and the idea of "feeling" a tailwhip without breaking anything, it's worth your attention.

Best for: Extreme-sports lovers, racing fans, anyone who enjoys trial-and-error in a virtual skatepark.

Sports, Racing, Simulation

Where to play: Steam →

Star Trek: Infection

7.8/10
Quest 3/3SPCVR/Steam Moderate $29.99

A Starfleet mission turns into a survival nightmare in VR.

Star Trek: Infection takes the franchise's classic iconography — narrow corridors, tricorders, doors hissing open — and stains it with body horror. It's less a shooting gallery and more an uneasy walk along the edges of canon. For Star Trek fans it can be a playable "what if," where the familiar universe is shot through with something dark and viscous.

Best for: Trekkies, fans of narrative horror, players who prefer tense exploration over constant action.

Horror, Survival, Narrative

Where to play: Meta Quest Store →Steam →
