Which VR headset should you buy in 2026?

There are more than half a dozen headsets worth owning in 2026, and the "best" one depends on what you want it for and what you'll use it with. Here's our short, honest take, organized by use case. For most people and first-timers: Meta Quest 3S or Quest 3

If this is your first time in VR, your headset is almost certainly a Meta Quest: they're standalone (no PC or console needed), have the largest game library, and are the easiest to use. The Quest 3S is the cheapest way into the ecosystem; the Quest 3 costs more but gives you sharper lenses and better mixed reality. Which one is right for you is broken down in our Quest 3 vs Quest 3S guide.

If you own a PS5: PlayStation VR2

It's the only headset that works with the PlayStation 5, so for console gamers the decision is simple. But there's fine print (no PC support, not standalone), and sometimes a Quest makes more sense even if you own a PS5. The full case is in Best VR for PS5.

For PCVR and the best image quality: Pimax and Bigscreen

If you have a gaming PC and want the best possible image, PCVR is another league. The Pimax Crystal Light is the community favorite for clarity per dollar; the Crystal Super steps fidelity up again; and the Bigscreen Beyond 2 is remarkably light. They all need a capable PC and some setup: what you actually need is in What you need for PCVR, and the honest take on Pimax is in Is the Pimax Crystal Light worth it?.

A Quest alternative: Pico 4 Ultra

The Pico 4 Ultra is standalone like the Quest, adds eye tracking, and also connects to a PC for wireless PCVR. It's the most interesting alternative if you want something other than Meta, though its app ecosystem is smaller.

And once you have it, take care of it

A well-kept headset lasts for years. The most important thing — and the one most owners don't know — is to never leave the lenses exposed to sunlight. How to clean them without ruining them is in this guide.
