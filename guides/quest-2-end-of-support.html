Is the Quest 2 still supported? What changes and when

Updated November 2026 · 4 min read

Yes, for now. Your Quest 2 keeps working, and so do the games you bought. What Meta announced in November 2026 is the end of new features: the last update that adds them, v2.13, reaches every headset by March 2027. Security updates continue until December 2027, and after that month no new apps will come out for it.

The dates

WhenWhat happens
January to March 2027v2.13 arrives, the last update with new features. The headset keeps everything it has.
Until December 2027Security updates and critical bug fixes, every quarter.
December 2027Last month to release new apps that support the Quest 2. After that, new games launch only for the Quest 3, 3S and newer headsets.
From 2028It still works: your games, the Store and PC VR over Link. It just stops getting security updates.

Sources: Meta's help page on the change, its developer blog and its security update policy.

What keeps working

The games and apps you own, solo and multiplayer. The Store stays open, so you can keep buying. Multiplayer with people on a Quest 3 or 3S continues, and developers can keep updating games that already support the Quest 2. Connecting it to a PC with Link continues too (how Link works).

What you lose

New features: whatever Meta adds to the Quest 3 and 3S from 2027 on won't reach the Quest 2. Some new games already skip it: Batman: Arkham Shadow, for example, only runs on the Quest 3 and 3S, and UploadVR keeps a list of others. After December 2027, no new game will come out for it at all. And without security updates from 2028, it's sensible to be careful with the browser and with the accounts you sign into on it.

Is a used Quest 2 still worth buying?

Only if it's very cheap and you already know which games you want. Used ones sell for about $100 to $150 in the US, according to the Flipify price tracker in November 2026. A new Quest 3S costs $349.99, and early Black Friday deals have put it at about $297.

The 3S uses the same lenses and the same screen as the Quest 2, so the picture looks much the same. What the extra money buys is the newer chip, mixed reality in color, every new game, and about two more years of support. For most people that's worth it; a Quest 2 makes sense as a cheap first try, or for someone who will only play games that already run on it. If you already have one

Keep using it: nothing stops working in 2027. Upgrade when a game you want only runs on the Quest 3 or 3S, or when you want mixed reality in color. Your purchases go with your Meta account, so games that support the newer headsets are there when you switch. If you sell it, remove it from your Meta account and reset it to factory settings first.
